Docs / Desktop pets

Desktop pets

A pet is one bot's own picture, sitting on the Mac's desktop with nothing around it. It is the whole window: no title bar, no panel, no background — the bot's face on your wallpaper, doing small things while you work, and answering a question when you click it.

Pets are macOS only, off by default, and switched on one bot at a time. A phone has no desktop to sit on; there the bot's picture in the bot list is the same idea, already done.

A pet is normally the bot's picture. It can also be the bot's own drawn face, if that bot has one — the Avatar tab in the bot's settings builds it (docs/APPS.md), and the pet draws whatever face the bot wears. Either way the pet prints the bot's state underneath it in one word — Idle, Thinking, Reading, Working, Using the computer, Waiting for you, Blocked, Done — so the desk tells you what the bot is doing without opening a window.

What a pet is

One pet, one window, one bot. The window is the icon's own 96 pt square plus a clear 12 pt margin the picture never draws into, and everything else about it is invisible: no frame, no coloured background, no shadow rectangle. What you see is the bot's face, with the desktop showing through everywhere the picture is transparent.

The clear parts are not clickable. A click on a corner of the pet's square goes to whatever is behind it — the desktop, another app's window — the way a click on empty space should. The click that reaches the pet is the one that lands on the picture. That is the difference between a pet on a desktop and a widget parked on it.

Putting one on the desktop

Open the bot's own settings and find Desktop pet, then turn the switch on. The switch is labelled with the bot's name — Show that bot on the desktop — and the pet appears straight away, at the centre of the screen you are on. Bring it here puts it back there later if you have dragged it somewhere and lost it.

  • Per bot. Each bot has its own switch, so the desk can carry one pet or five.
  • Off by default. Nothing appears until you turn it on for that bot.
  • Per Mac. Which pets are on this desk is a property of this desk. It is stored in the app's own settings, not on the host, so a second Mac starts with no pets and nothing follows you between them.
  • They come back at launch. Quitting Hermey takes the pets away without touching the switches; the next launch puts them back where they were.

What it does while you leave it alone

The pet is not a still image. On its own clock it does one of four things — a small bob, a tilt, a short hop, or a wander of a few points — every 8 to 30 seconds, chosen at random so two pets on one desk never move in step. It blinks every 3 to 9 seconds, and the whole face drifts a few points left and right every couple of seconds, which is what makes a still picture read as looking around.

Nothing is drawn over the art: the movement is all transform on the bot's own picture, so a bot with a custom face needs no second animation asset.

With Reduce Motion on in macOS the pet calms down rather than stopping: the bob is halved, the hops, tilts and wander are dropped, the drifting stops, and the blink becomes a gentler squash.

Clicking it: the balloon

Click the pet and a frosted speech balloon opens beside it — above and to one side, never over the icon, so the pet stays visible while you type. The tail points at the pet, and the balloon picks a seat that fits the display: above-right by default, above-left when the right edge would run off the screen, centred above, below when there is no room above, and beside it on a short display.

  • Type, then Return. The question goes to that bot. One question at a time: the field is disabled while a turn is in flight.
  • Thinking. The balloon says Thinking… until the answer arrives.
  • The answer grows to a cap, then scrolls. The balloon is sized to its content up to a ceiling; a long reply scrolls inside it instead of covering the screen. Copy puts the text on the clipboard (and the button says Copied).
  • Dismissing is not resetting. Escape, or a click anywhere else, closes the balloon. Click the pet again and the last exchange is still there. New is the explicit reset: it clears the balloon and anything in flight and leaves an empty field ready.
  • Two minutes is the timeout. A pet's question is a turn of its own addressed to the bot, marked as coming from the desktop pet (POST /v1/bots/{profile}/ask with from: desktop-pet), so it does not have to go through the conversation you have open in the window. If the host has not answered in two minutes the balloon says Sent — it hasn't answered yet. with Retry.

The badge, and the hop you did not see

The pet shows the same unread count the bot list does — the app's own number, not a second tally.

  • A reply lands while Hermey is minimised or hidden and the pet hops and puts a badge on the icon's top-right corner: a dot for one turn waiting, the number for more, 9+ past nine. The hop is the pet telling you something arrived while you were not looking. If you can see the window, nothing hops — you are already reading it.
  • The badge is the count, shown. Nothing pops up by itself; the balloon waits for your click.
  • Clicking the pet opens the balloon and clears the badge, because reading the answer is reading the bot — the bot list's dot goes at the same time.

Moving it, closing it, and where it lives

  • Drag it anywhere. The position is remembered per bot and per screen, so a pet can live on the second display and stay there. Unplug the display or change the resolution and the pet is moved back onto a screen you can see, at the next launch and when the screen setup changes.
  • Right-click → Close pet. The window closes and the bot's Desktop pet switch goes off with it, so the settings toggle is the way back. A pet closed this way comes back centred when you switch it on again.
  • It floats. The pet is above normal windows and follows you across Spaces; over a full-screen app macOS may hide it, which is the platform's call rather than the app's.

Troubleshooting

What you see What it means
No pet after switching it on Check you are looking at the right screen — press Bring it here in that bot's settings. macOS only: the pet does not exist on iOS.
The pet is not where you left it The display it was on is gone or its resolution changed. It is moved onto a visible screen; drag it where you want it and it is remembered again.
A click beside the pet does nothing By design: only the picture takes clicks, and the clear corners of its square pass the click to the desktop. Click the face.
Can't reach the host right now. The app cannot reach hermeyd. Check the Mac is still paired and the host is up — the pet itself keeps drawing.
Sent — it hasn't answered yet. The question reached the bot and it had not answered by the timeout. Retry re-sends the same question.
The pet answers but you cannot find the answer later The pet keeps its last exchange only while it is on screen, and nothing is written to disk. A pet's transcript is not a document.
Two pets, one desk Fine, and supported: each has its own window, position and question.

The design it was built from

docs/DESKTOP-PETS.md is the design record: the window behaviour, the decisions worth stating, the risks, and the corrections the build forced. It is written for whoever changes the feature, not for whoever uses it.
